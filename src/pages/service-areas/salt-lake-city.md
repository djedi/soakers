## What's different about installing a spa in Salt Lake City

**Older homes and electrical service.** Many Salt Lake City neighborhoods have older homes, and older homes are more likely to have smaller electrical panels. A full-size 240V spa needs a dedicated circuit, and HomeGuide's national cost data puts a panel upgrade at $1,400–$2,800 when one is needed. Before you fall in love with a big spa, take a photo of your electrical panel and bring it in. If an upgrade doesn't make sense, a plug-and-play [Garden Spa](/spa-models/garden-spas/) or [Nordic ALL-IN-110V](/spa-models/nordic-all-in-110v/) runs on a standard 120V outlet. Our [110V vs 240V guide](/guides/110v-vs-240v-hot-tubs/) explains the trade-offs.

**Tight lots and access.** Narrow side yards, detached garages and mature trees are common in established neighborhoods. Measure the narrowest point between the street and your backyard. Compact spas like the Nordic Bella MS (34.5 inches wide) were designed to get through tight spots.

**Historic districts.** If your home is in one of Salt Lake City's local historic districts, check with the city's planning division before adding exterior structures like a pergola or gazebo enclosure.

**Winter inversions.** Valley inversions can settle in for days. Many city owners soak in the evening or right after a storm clears the air. Our [Utah winter guide](/guides/hot-tub-utah-winter/) covers cold-weather care.
