## Draper hot tubs, from the valley to the ridge

Draper covers a wide range of terrain, from flat valley neighborhoods near I-15 to hillside lots in Corner Canyon and SunCrest. Central Draper is about 9 miles from our Midvale showroom, roughly 14 minutes north on I-15. Come in and try the display models before you decide what will work in your yard.

## Planning around Draper terrain

**Hillside and sloped lots.** Many Draper homes sit on slopes. A hot tub needs a level, solid base. On a slope that usually means a poured pad, a retaining edge, or a deck engineered for the load. Plan this early, because it's often the longest part of the project.

**Delivery access.** Steep driveways, retaining walls and narrow side yards affect how a spa gets to its spot. Measure the narrowest point. HomeGuide notes that a small share of deliveries need a crane, typically $300–$1,500 nationally. We'd rather plan the route with you ahead of time than discover a problem on delivery day.

**SunCrest elevation.** SunCrest sits up on Traverse Ridge at roughly 6,000 feet, about 1,500 feet above central Draper. That means colder nights, more snow and more wind. For a spa up there, prioritize:

- Strong insulation, like full foam on [Island Spas](/spa-models/island-spas/) or the Nordic Wrap option
- A heavy, tight-fitting [cover](/covers/) with lockable straps for wind
- A 240V heater for faster recovery (see [110V vs 240V](/guides/110v-vs-240v-hot-tubs/))

**HOAs.** SunCrest has an owners association, and many other Draper subdivisions have HOAs too. Check what approval you need for a spa, screening, or a [Covana](/covana/) enclosure before you order.
