## Planning a hot tub in Sandy

**Slopes on the east side.** Sandy rises from the valley floor toward the Wasatch foothills, so many east-side yards slope. A hot tub has to sit perfectly level on a solid base, usually a concrete pad, compacted gravel, or a deck engineered for the weight. A filled spa with people in it can weigh 3,000–5,000+ pounds. On a sloped lot, sort out the pad before you schedule delivery.

**HOAs and neighbors.** Many Sandy subdivisions have HOAs with rules about exterior changes, screening, or setbacks. Check your CC&Rs before you buy, especially if you're considering a [Covana](/covana/) automated cover, which rises into a gazebo-style enclosure.

**Electrical.** Most full-size spas need a dedicated 240V circuit. HomeGuide's 2025 national data puts a typical new spa circuit at $800–$1,600. Our [110V vs 240V guide](/guides/110v-vs-240v-hot-tubs/) explains when a plug-and-play spa makes sense instead.

**Canyon-side winters.** Neighborhoods closer to the mountains tend to see colder nights and more snow than the valley floor. Look for strong insulation and a heavy-duty cover. See our [winter guide](/guides/hot-tub-utah-winter/).
