## Planning a hot tub in West Jordan

**New construction and unfinished yards.** If your home is new or the backyard isn't finished, you're in the best position to add a spa affordably. Before sod and sprinklers go in:

- Pick the spa location and pour a level pad (or prepare a compacted gravel base)
- Have an electrician run conduit for a 240V circuit if you'll want a full-size spa. Trenching is cheaper before landscaping
- Keep a delivery path open. Fences and gates are much easier to plan around than to take down later

HomeGuide's national cost data puts a concrete spa pad at $500–$2,500 and a new spa circuit at $800–$1,600. Our [Utah cost guide](/guides/hot-tub-cost-utah/) breaks down the full budget.

**HOA rules.** Many West Jordan subdivisions have HOAs. Check rules on exterior structures, screening and fencing, especially before adding a [Covana](/covana/) automated cover, which rises into a gazebo-style enclosure.

**Wind and open lots.** Newer neighborhoods often have young trees and open views, so your spa may sit exposed. A cover with locking straps and a sturdy [cover lifter](/covers/) handle wind better, and a privacy screen can help with neighbors.
